How the index is actually computed

SCITI is a two-layer model. First, structured research assigns seven pillar scores from auditable outcome indicators, evidence items, and field verification. Second, deterministic mathematics aggregates those pillar scores into a composite score, tier, and contribution breakdown.

1. Analytical Structure

The critical methodological distinction is between research judgment and deterministic aggregation.

  • Research layer: each city receives seven pillar scores from a structured reading of public indicators, evidence items, and field verification.
  • Aggregation layer: once those seven inputs are fixed, the composite score, weighted contribution bars, and tier assignment are fully reproducible from code.
  • Confidence layer: evidence quality is reported separately so uncertainty stays visible instead of being hidden inside the score.

SCITI therefore does not claim that one API feed or one normalization function can fully explain urban life. It claims something narrower and stronger: the final aggregation step is exact, while the research step is explicit about where judgment remains necessary.

2. Composite Mathematics

The composite score is a weighted average over seven pillars.

Composite = Σ(pillar score × weight) / 100
Pillar Weight Interpretive Role
Livability 25% Daily urban function: housing, mobility, utilities, and whether life works without friction.
Economy 20% Productive capacity, income, output, and honest demand absorption.
Safety 15% Crime exposure, emergency readiness, and practical security.
Wellbeing 15% Depth of public-service capacity after the ribbon-cutting.
Environment 10% Air, water, green cover, and ecological resilience.
Hospitality 10% Belonging, civic warmth, cultural life, and whether people want to stay.
Digital 5% Enabling infrastructure: data rails, IoT, and digital service use that changes outcomes.

The weights sum to 100. Livability carries the largest share because a city must function for residents before it can meaningfully claim to be smart. Digital carries the smallest share because technology is an enabler, not the goal itself.

3. Data Confidence Mathematics

Confidence is reported separately from the composite score. It is designed to answer a different question: how dense and fresh is the evidentiary backbone supporting this dossier?

Confidence = weighted average(core coverage 65%, extended coverage 15%, provenance density 10%, freshness 10%)
  • Core coverage (65%): six core indicators are tracked across the repo-level city metric model — GPP per capita, average monthly income, annual PM2.5, hospital beds per 10K, crime per 100K, and green coverage.
  • Extended coverage (15%): seven additional indicators widen the baseline — GPP growth, PM2.5 trend, water quality, forest coverage, FDI inflow, industry composition, and labor force.
  • Provenance density (10%): the model rewards cities whose score narrative is backed by a thicker stack of observations and source-linked supports.
  • Freshness (10%): observations are weighted against the fixed release cut-off of 2026-04-04 rather than against a moving runtime date, keeping the release reproducible.

The confidence label is then classified as High, Medium, or Low. Crucially, this label does not secretly alter the composite score. It stays visible as a separate signal.

5. Treatment of Thinly Observed Cities

Promotion-zone and registered cities with insufficient evidence are not dressed up as fully observed cases. In the repository they receive provisional low-confidence baselines and are clearly marked as such. This preserves rank continuity while making their uncertainty explicit.

That is an uncomfortable but methodologically cleaner choice than pretending the missing evidence does not matter.

6. What the Method Does Not Claim

  • No hidden caps, prestige bonuses, or bespoke city penalties in the aggregation step.
  • No claim that a single normalization pipeline fully explains city quality.
  • No claim that every listed source is used equally for every city.
  • No promise that scores say anything about events after the release cut-off.

Those limits matter. A methodology becomes more credible, not less, when it is precise about what is automated, what is curated, and what remains uncertain.
